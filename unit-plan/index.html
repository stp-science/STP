The framework behind the planning

Understanding Niho Taniwha

Niho Taniwha is a Māori teaching and learning model explored by Dr Melanie Riwai-Couch in Niho Taniwha: Improving Teaching and Learning for Ākonga Māori. It supports culturally responsive practice that improves Māori learners’ experiences and success, including language, identity, culture and wellbeing alongside achievement.

The model brings together Whai, Ako and Mau, with Tipu as the growth these phases seek to achieve. Both teachers and learners are part of the learning process. The name refers to a traditional tukutuku pattern representing the teeth of a taniwha.

How we use it at STP

The links above are STP’s planning application of the framework. They help us connect purpose, informed teaching, evidence and improvement. They are not exact translations or a claim that each part belongs in only one box.

Intent · Whai

Be clear about the learning, its purpose and whose needs and aspirations inform it.

Implementation · Ako + Mau

Learn from relevant knowledge and perspectives, enact the curriculum and check learning.

Impact · Mau + Tipu

Use evidence to evaluate learning and decide what to retain, strengthen or change.

Across the whole plan: consider whose perspectives are included, what barriers may affect participation and learning, and what learners and whānau tell us about their experiences. Keep high expectations and make meaningful connections to people, culture and place.

Niho Taniwha guides the thinking behind the plan. Adding Māori headings, a whakataukī or a local example alone does not establish culturally responsive practice. Use the prompts to inform decisions and review their effects.

The Māori learner focus remains central when we apply this lens across all subjects. The unit plan records the intended curriculum; lesson planning sets out teaching and practice; assessment and review show what needs to happen next.
